## 1. How FIFA/EA FC, PES/eFootball and Football Manager structure team AI (positioning, runs, personalities, set pieces, GK)

### Takeaway
EA FC's current model (FC IQ, since FC 25) separates *formation* (defensive shape, where players return when the ball is lost) from *roles* (off-ball behaviour in possession), and layers team-level presets on top: build-up style (short/balanced/counter), defensive approach (deep/balanced/high/aggressive with an explicit 1-100 line height), and named tactical identities (Tiki-Taka, Long Ball, Counter Attack, Wing Play, Park the Bus, Pressing, Kick and Rush). FC 26 career mode assigns each AI manager a "Tactical Vision" so opponents play differently. This is a directly copyable data model for AFC.

### Cited Findings
- FC IQ is "the new core tactical and positional system" in FC 25, built on three components: Player Roles, Team Tactics and Smart Tactics. — [EA Pitch Notes: FC 25 FC IQ Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive)
- Formation controls defensive shape: when possession is lost, players return to formation positions; tactical settings, role familiarity and focus determine how quickly. Roles control off-ball behaviour in possession and let players leave formation spots. — [EA FC IQ Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive)
- Build-Up Styles: Short Passing (players come short to support the carrier, "patient" transition), Balanced (mix of forward runs and coming short, "normal"), Counter (forward runs behind the defence, "quick"). — [EA FC IQ Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive)
- Defensive Approaches with explicit line-height ranges: Deep 1-30 (default 25, line drops with runs, no pressure); Balanced 31-60 (50, flexible tracking); High 61-90 (70, rarely tracks runs, minimal pressure); Aggressive 91-100 (95, never tracks runs, presses on heavy touch, extra stamina drain). — [EA FC IQ Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive)
- Pressing trigger example: the Aggressive approach presses "on heavy touch" (i.e. a pressing trigger tied to a poor first touch) at the cost of stamina. — [EA FC IQ Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive)
- Role Familiarity (base, Role+, Role++, Out of Position) changes run types/timing/spot selection, marking reaction time and distance, press tightness, run tracking and transition speed ("The lower the Familiarity the longer it takes for players to transition"); familiarity accounts for 10-40% of positioning calculations depending on context; out-of-position players "may slack on their attacking duties." — [EA FC IQ Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive)
- Role counts at launch conflict across EA copy (31 roles with 1-3 focuses = 52 combinations vs "52 different Player Roles"). — [EA FC IQ Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive) (as summarised by search; conflicting phrasing)
- Smart Tactics suggests tactics in real time from scoreline, match importance, time, red cards, possession and xG; suggested set = Balanced, Tiki-Taka, Long Ball, Counter Attack, Wing Play, Park the Bus, Pressing. Tactic changes apply gradually ("Tactical Change in Progress"), faster when the ball is out of play. Quick Tactics: Offside Trap, Team Press, Overload Set Pieces, Get In Box. — [EA FC IQ Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive)
- FC 26 career: AI managers "will try to instill their Tactical Vision on the club," which "will change how opposition teams will play"; visions named are Balanced, High Pressing, Possession, Wing Play, Counter Attack, Park the Bus, Kick and Rush (e.g. PSG under Luis Enrique: "Dominant tactic: High Pressing"). Clubs prefer managers whose style matches theirs. — [EA Pitch Notes: FC 26 Career Mode Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/pitch-notes-fc26-career-mode-deep-dive)
- FC 26 AI lineup selection depends on match importance and form; AI can field "a slightly weaker lineup in the early stages of a cup competition, or after they've clinched their league objectives." — [EA FC 26 Career Mode Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/pitch-notes-fc26-career-mode-deep-dive)
- FC 26 "Authentic Gameplay" (default for career) is "more tactical and methodical," favouring "patient build-up, smart positioning," and "can feel less predictable"; it exposes options for "AI defending, player reactions, physics, and fatigue impact" and sliders for "the balance of assistance and error" from shooting to defending. — [EA FC 26 Career Mode Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/pitch-notes-fc26-career-mode-deep-dive)
- FC 26 TU 1.4.0 (Jan 2026) reduced AI defenders' reaction times when marking the ball carrier, and made Squad Battles CPU "more proactive and attacking" and less focused on keeping possession. — [EA Pitch Notes TU 1.4.0](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/pitch-notes-fc26-title-update-1-4-0); [PSU summary](https://www.psu.com/news/ea-sports-fc-26-update-1-4-0-brings-tweaks-to-ai-defending-career-mode-more/)
- Football Manager: official FM23 material says AI managers make "more intelligent tactical tweaks as a match changes"; FM27 staff inform you of tactical changes the opposition manager makes in-match. Community/official-insight view is that the AI does not "learn" your specific tactic; it reacts to your reputation and league position, and different AI managers with different tactics beat you in different ways. A 2026 fan blog claims FM26 detects repeated patterns, unconfirmed by official pages. — [FM Scout: can the FM AI figure out your tactic](https://www.fmscout.com/a-can-the-football-manager-ai-figure-out-your-tactic.html); [FM27 Tactical Tweaks](https://www.footballmanager.com/node/1108); [FM Scout tactic longevity Q&A](https://www.fmscout.com/q-21381-Tactic-longevity.html); [footballmanagerblog (unverified)](https://www.footballmanagerblog.org/2026/01/fm26-new-ai-coach-algorithm-forces.html)
- Classic textbook approach ("Programming Game AI by Example", Simple Soccer chapter): team-level state machine (attacking/defending), a `SupportSpotCalculator::DetermineBestSupportingPosition` that scores candidate spots so a "supporting attacker" moves to the best place to receive a pass from the controlling player. — [O'Reilly ch. 77](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/programming-game-ai/9781556220784/chapter-77.html); [ch. 78](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/programming-game-ai/9781556220784/chapter-78.html); [community port](https://github.com/luiskarlos/soccer); [Middlesex lecture notes](https://cwa.mdx.ac.uk/cmt3325/lect8Approx/soccerAI.html)

### Inferences
- A per-team "tactical vision" record (build-up style, line height 1-100, press trigger, width, run tendency) plus formation is the industry-standard structure, and it maps almost 1:1 onto GRF's existing team-tactic properties (Section 3) and onto AFC's GDScript `team_ai.gd`.
- Using scoreline/time-driven tactic switching (Smart Tactics; FM in-match tweaks) for AI teams gives visible "the opponent changed approach" moments, which reads as intelligence, not cheating.
- Rotating weaker lineups in early cup rounds is a cheap, legitimate way to vary difficulty in a career season.

## 2. Difficulty: what changes across levels, DDA, perceived fairness, and what players hate

### Takeaway
EA has never published an official per-level table; fan-compiled tables say difficulty mainly raises AI "reaction speed," attacking/defending intelligence and marking, not raw attributes. EA formally denies DDA/scripting in FIFA/FC despite owning a DDA patent, and the long-running "scripting" suspicion shows how quickly players attribute losses to hidden cheating. Current FC 26/27 complaints cluster on: CPU winning tackles instantly, zero CPU fouls, AI "anticipating my input," Legendary being either trivially passive or superhuman, and low-rated teams playing like elite sides.

### Cited Findings
- Fan-compiled (not official) tables list four difficulty-linked AI stats: reaction speed, attacking intelligence, defending intelligence, marking/space; for FIFA 20, reaction speed rises from 20% (Beginner) to 80% (World Class) and 90% (Legendary). FC 25 uses the same four. — [FIFPlay FIFA 20 Difficulty Levels](https://www.fifplay.com/fifa-20-difficulty-levels/); [fifauteam FC 25 Difficulty Levels](https://fifauteam.com/fifa-25-difficulty-levels-explained/)
- EA states it does not use "any scripting or 'Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment' (DDA)" in FIFA, Madden or NHL Ultimate Team; it owns a DDA patent but says the tech "never was in FIFA, Madden or NHL, and never will be." — [EA: Fair Play & DDA](https://ea.com/amp/news/fair-play-and-dynamic-difficulty-adjustment); [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/fifa-19/scripting-dda-momentum)
- A 2020 class action alleging DDA in FUT was dropped after plaintiffs got "detailed technical information and access to speak with our engineers." — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/fifa-dynamic-difficulty-lawsuit-dropped-after-plaintiffs-talk-to-eas-engineers); critics noted the patent language sounds close to "scripting" — [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/ea-granted-patent-dynamic-difficulty-adjustment-technology-2915676)
- FC 26 offline-vs-CPU forum complaints: CPU wins tackles almost instantly, match-end stats show zero CPU fouls/cards; claims that higher difficulty adds more "CPU cheating." — [EA forums: offline gameplay vs CPU – fouls](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-26-feedback-en/re-feedback--offline-gameplay-vs-cpu---fouls/12719361); [anyone been fouled yet](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-26-feedback-en/anyone-been-fouled-yet-even-after-1-1-0/12830110)
- FC 26: "The AI players in Legendary difficulty anticipate my action before execution" (input-reading complaint). — [EA forums thread](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-26-feedback-en/the-ai-players-in-legendary-difficulty-anticipate-my-action-before-execution/13590059)
- Opposite complaint: Legendary "way too easy," AI doesn't press or adapt; on top difficulties the career AI rarely attacks and passes around the back without intent. — [Legendary in FC26 is way too easy](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-26-feedback-en/legendary-in-ea-fc26-is-way-too-easy/12863523); [Career difficulty doesn't work](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-26-feedback-en/re-career-difficulty-level-doesn%e2%80%99t-work-all-are-very-easy-/12865725)
- FC 27: lowering Legendary to Professional did not visibly reduce CPU accuracy or ball retention; "World Class difficulty is an embarrassment," lower-rated teams play like Champions League sides. — [Severe gameplay issues and unfair AI behaviour (FC 27)](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-27-career-mode-en/severe-gameplay-issues-and-unfair-ai-behavior/13872849); [World Class embarrassment (FC 27)](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-27-feedback-en/world-class-difficulty-is-an-embarrassment-to-gameplay-balance-in-fc-27/13826809)
- Dream League Soccer (mobile) complaints found were mostly matchmaking/lag/ads; one AI complaint: defenders "rush forward and leave easy goals," i.e. predictability. FTGames says the game balances fun with realism rather than strict simulation. — [Sikayetvar: DLS defenders rush forward](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/dream-league-soccer-us/dream-league-soccer-defenders-rush-forward-and-leave-easy-goals); [FTGames support article](https://support.ftgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000842697-Something-happened-in-the-game-that-s-not-realistic-Why-is-this)
- eFootball complaints are mainly about PvP "momentum" and cheaters, not offline CPU. — [Sikayetvar eFootball momentum](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/efootball-us/i-keep-losing-in-efootball-2025-due-to-unfair-momentum-and-cheaters-using-vpn-tricks)
- Research: DDA via classic heuristic parameter tuning "often turns out to be very noticeable, e.g. as 'rubber-banding'"; players preferred a deep-player-behaviour-model DDA. — [Newcastle OpenLab: Enemy Within](https://openlab.ncl.ac.uk/research/enemy-within-long-term-motivation-effects-of-deep-player-behavior-models-for-dynamic-difficulty-adjustment)
- DDA "can decrease the self-efficacy of less skilled players and can be perceived as unfair by skilled players." — [Mantz et al., CEUR GMAP](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4206/GMAP-3.pdf)
- Perceived fairness of balancing depends on how it is implemented and the context. — [CHI 2024, doi 10.1145/3613904.3642441](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642441)
- Weaker players reported lower perceived difficulty with DDA (Colwell's Castle Defence). — [arXiv 1806.04471](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.04471); rubber-banding systems "can thus suffer from disbalance and exploitability" — [arXiv 2012.03309](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03309)

### Inferences
- What players read as "cheating" is mostly *perfect* behaviour (instant tackles, zero fouls, perfect anticipation, every 50/50 won), not high difficulty per se. A fair hard AI should be good at *decisions* (positioning, pass choice, press timing) while still committing fouls, misplacing passes and losing duels at attribute-plausible rates.
- Hidden, outcome-based adjustment (rubber-banding/"scripting") is the most distrusted mechanism; if AFC uses any adaptation, make it transparent (e.g. a visible "Adaptive" difficulty option, or suggesting a lower level after N heavy losses) rather than silently changing stats mid-match.
- Since even EA cannot dispel scripting accusations, AFC should avoid any mechanism whose effect is "the AI gets stronger when behind" that is not explained by visible tactics (all-out attack, subs).

- No official EA per-level parameter table exists in sources found; fan tables are unverified.
- Hunicke's 2005 "Hamlet" DDA paper and Missura & Gärtner were not fetched.
- No Reddit-specific evidence gathered (quota exhausted); EA forum posts are anecdotal.

## 3. Google Research Football engine: what `difficulty` scales, built-in AI behaviour, weaknesses, Kaggle and TiZero

### Takeaway
In the GRF engine source, the per-team difficulty `d` is not just a "reaction speed" knob as the paper suggests: it (a) multiplies EVERY player attribute of that team by `0.3 + 0.7*d`, (b) adds `(1-d)*100 ms` to each AI-controlled player's reaction time, and (c) scales the AI's "hunt"/pressing distance by `0.3 + 0.7*d`. So low difficulty is effectively a large attribute nerf, and it applies to the team, including human-controlled players' stats. Separately, the built-in team AI already has score/time/possession-driven offensiveness and a full set of tactic sliders that AFC can use to make teams distinct. The built-in bot is known to be exploitable via offside, and TiZero is much stronger than built-in hard but mishandles offside passes.

### Cited Findings (engine source, read directly)
- Difficulty is set per team: `match.cpp:119-124` passes `right_team_difficulty` / `left_team_difficulty` into each `Team`; `team.hpp:62 GetAiDifficulty()`. — [GRF engine source](https://github.com/google-research/football/tree/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src) (local: `.../src/onthepitch/match.cpp`, `team.hpp`)
- Attribute scaling, `player.cpp:452-457`:
  ```
  float Player::GetStat(PlayerStat name) const {
    float multiplier = 0.3f + 0.7f * team->GetAiDifficulty();
    multiplier *= 0.7f + 0.3f * GetFatigueFactorInv();
    return playerData->GetStat(name) * multiplier;
  }
  ```
  i.e. at d=0.3 all stats are x0.51, at d=0.6 x0.72, at d=0.95 x0.965, at d=1.0 x1.0 (before fatigue). This is `Player::GetStat`, used for every player of the team regardless of who controls them. — [GRF player.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/player.cpp)
- Reaction time: base `IController::GetReactionTime_ms() = round(80 - physical_reaction*40)` ms (`icontroller.cpp:30-32`, and physical_reaction is itself difficulty-scaled), and AI `PlayerController` adds `(1 - d) * 100` ms (`playercontroller.cpp:61-65`). — [GRF playercontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/playercontroller.cpp)
- Pressing/hunting: in `elizacontroller.cpp:425-429`, `huntDistanceThreshold = 10 + (1 - mindSet)*10` metres, reduced by fatigue/current speed, then multiplied by `0.3 + d*0.7`, so low-difficulty AI chases the ball carrier from much shorter range. — [GRF elizacontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp)
- Built-in team tactics (`teamAIcontroller.cpp:56-81`): base values `position_offense_depth_factor 0.9`, `position_defense_depth_factor 0.75`, `position_offense_width_factor 0.9`, `position_defense_width_factor 0.8`, `position_offense_ownhalf_factor 0.52`, `position_defense_ownhalf_factor 0.54`, midfield focus 0.6/0.5 (strength 0.35/0.35), side focus strength 0.1/0.4, micro focus strength 0.7/0.8; per-team user tactics from team data can offset each by +/- a multiplier (0.1 for depth/width, 0.3 for midfield focus, 0.15 for micro focus). `AIfunctions.cpp` also reads `dribble_offensiveness` and `dribble_centermagnet` user properties. Team tactic properties are loaded from team data (`teamdata.cpp:265-271`). — [GRF teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp)
- Game-state adaptation already built in (`teamAIcontroller.cpp:1221-1250`, recomputed every second): `goalFactor = clamp(0.5 + (oppGoals - goals)*0.25, 0, 1)`; `timeFactor = 0.5 + 0.5*clamp(matchTime/6,300,000 ms, 0, 1)`; `offenseBias = 0.5 + (goalFactor-0.5)*timeFactor`; blended 50/50 with a recent-60-seconds possession bias into `offensivenessBias`, which drives how high the defensive block holds (`startDistance = 30 + 20*offensivenessBias` metres from goal; fall-back `offsetX = 20 + 10*(1-offensivenessBias)`). So trailing late makes the bot push up; leading makes it sit deeper. — [GRF teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp)
- Attacking runs: `SelectAttackingRunPlayer` picks the teammate closest to a point 26 m ahead of the ball carrier (excluding the carrier). Off-ball strategies exist per line: `default_def`, `default_mid`, `default_off`, `goalie_default`. — [GRF teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp); [strategies/offtheball](https://github.com/google-research/football/tree/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/strategies/offtheball)

### Cited Findings (papers / external)
- GRF paper: opponent is a rule-based bot whose difficulty θ in [0,1] speeds up or slows down reaction time and decision making; presets easy θ=0.05, medium θ=0.6, hard θ=0.95. — [Kurach et al., arXiv 1907.11180](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.11180); a 2023 study instead gives medium = 0.5 as default — [arXiv 2305.09458](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.09458) (conflict; the original paper says 0.6)
- The built-in AI has no rule against moving into offside positions; learned policies win possession by luring it offside; its logic "can be exploited easily." — [Agent-Centric Representations for MARL, arXiv 2104.09402](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09402)
- Agents trained vs the hard bot learned to charge forward so the bot ends offside; the hard bot "sometimes does not recognize offside cases and has considerable defensive vulnerability." — [arXiv 2305.09458](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.09458)
- TiZero (AAMAS 2023): 11v11 full game, tabula rasa via curriculum learning + self-play, no demonstrations; TrueSkill 45.2 vs TiKick 37.2, WeKick 30.9; first on a public evaluation platform with 95.8% win rate (score 9.7, collected 28 Oct 2022); claims >30% higher win rates than prior systems. — [TiZero, arXiv 2302.07515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07515); [AAMAS PDF](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~eg/AAMAS2023/pdfs/p67.pdf)
- MADRID diagnostics found TiZero "frequently falls victim to erroneously passing the ball to players unmistakably in offside positions." — [arXiv 2401.13460](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13460v3)
- Kaggle GRF 2020 (1,000+ teams; agents control one player: the carrier in attack or nearest defender in defence); winner WeKick (Tencent) used imitation learning, multi-head value and distributed league training; TiKick later built full-game multi-agent play from WeKick's single-agent demonstrations. — [TiKick, arXiv 2110.04507](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.04507); [Self-play survey, arXiv 2408.01072](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.01072); [secondary writeup](https://besthub.dev/articles/how-tencent-s-wekick-ai-dominated-the-google-football-kaggle-competition-1ffa586cc281)
- Beating the built-in AI at difficulty 1.0 in 11v11 is used as a research benchmark. — [arXiv 2503.13077](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13077)

### Current AFC wiring (local code, for the report writer)
- Levels: `["Amateur","Pro","World Class","Legendary","Ultimate"]`, engine away difficulty `AWAY_DIFF = [0.3, 0.6, 0.95, 1.0, 1.0]`, Legendary/Ultimate use TiZero weights when present (`engine_match_view.gd:36-37`, `settings_panel.gd:7-9`).
- Human (home) team is always created with `difficulty_home: 0.95` (`engine_match_view.gd:252`), so by `Player::GetStat` the user's players run at x0.965 stats while a 1.0 opponent (Legendary/Ultimate fallback) runs at x1.0; at World Class both are 0.95.
- GDScript sim: CPU difficulty changes pressing rate `lerp(0.9, 2.2, d)`, tackle chance `0.05*dt*d`, think interval multiplier `lerp(1.25, 0.85, d)`, skill-move chance `*(0.5+d)`, and error multiplier `lerp(1.6, 0.85, d)` (`team_ai.gd:260,264,437,509`; `match_sim.gd:983`).

### Inferences
- Because GRF's top-level difficulty is mostly a global attribute multiplier, "Ultimate/Legendary" at d=1.0 against a user at 0.95 gives the CPU ~3.6% better stats on every attribute and ~5 ms faster AI reactions for its players, on top of TiZero's far stronger policy. This plausibly contributes to users finding AFC "hard," and is the kind of attribute edge players call cheating. Setting `difficulty_home` to 1.0 (or equal to the away value) removes an invisible handicap on the user.
- At Amateur (0.3), the CPU plays at about half its stats: very slow, poor passes. A large step to Pro (0.72x) and World Class (0.965x). The curve is steep between Pro and World Class; adding intermediate values (e.g. 0.75-0.85) would smooth it.
- Better levers than stat multipliers for "fair" difficulty in GRF: the hunt distance and reaction delay (decision quality), plus team tactic sliders (line depth/width, offensivenessBias) — these change behaviour without giving superhuman attributes.
- TiZero's offside-pass weakness and the built-in bot's offside blindness mean a skilled user can exploit both by holding a high line; for TiZero levels, a rule-based post-filter that vetoes passes to offside receivers would make it look smarter.
- TiZero was trained against itself/GRF bots, not humans on touch controls; its 95.8% platform win rate implies it should be reserved for the top tier only.

- Could not confirm the exact difficulty values in the Kaggle `11_vs_11_kaggle` scenario or which GRF scenario uses 0.5 vs 0.6 for "medium" (Python scenario files not fetched).
- Did not analyse `goalie_default` keeper logic or set-piece handling in the engine.
- No Kaggle writeups on tuning rule-based GRF bots (e.g. popular public "memory patterns" rule-based kernels) were retrieved.

## 4. Teammate AI for the human's team (support runs, auto-switching, avoiding static teammates)

### Takeaway
Evidence gathered is limited (search quota ran out). The transferable principles come from FC IQ (roles govern off-ball runs; low familiarity slows transitions) and the textbook support-spot approach; in GRF, the human team's AI teammates are driven by the same team AI and their quality is also tied to the human team's difficulty value.

### Cited Findings
- In FC 25, roles dictate off-ball runs (run types, timing, spot selection), and Build-Up Style decides whether players come short or run in behind. — [EA FC IQ Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive)
- Simple Soccer computes a best supporting spot for one "supporting attacker" so a passing option is always available to the controlling player. — [O'Reilly ch. 77](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/programming-game-ai/9781556220784/chapter-77.html)
- GRF selects one attacking-run player per possession (closest teammate to a point 26 m ahead of the carrier). — [GRF teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp)
- In GRF, every player's stats (including the human team's) are scaled by `0.3+0.7*d` of their own team, and AI-controlled players get `(1-d)*100` ms extra reaction. — [GRF player.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/player.cpp)

### Inferences
- "Teammates standing still" usually means only one designated runner plus formation-return. Fix ideas: guarantee at least two support options (one short, one in behind) driven by build-up style; increase runner count when the team chooses Counter.
- The human team's difficulty in GRF should stay at 1.0 so user-side AI teammates get full reactions and pressing range.

### Gaps
- No sources retrieved on FC/eFootball auto-switching heuristics, Run Assist, or "teammate contain" settings; no Reddit data on DLS/eFootball teammate complaints.

## 5. Making AI teams feel distinct (possession, counter, long ball, high press) across a career

### Takeaway
Both EA (Tactical Visions per AI manager) and FM (different managers beat you differently; in-match tweaks) rely on per-manager/per-club style identities, not difficulty, to create variety. GRF already exposes the knobs needed to express these identities.

### Cited Findings
- FC 26 Tactical Visions: Balanced, High Pressing, Possession, Wing Play, Counter Attack, Park the Bus, Kick and Rush; AI managers impose them on their clubs, which changes how opponents play. — [EA FC 26 Career Mode Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/pitch-notes-fc26-career-mode-deep-dive)
- FC 25 Smart Tactics presets and situational triggers (scoreline, time, red cards, possession, xG). — [EA FC IQ Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive)
- FM: opponents change approach based on your reputation/league position; different managers' tactics and squads produce variety. — [FM Scout](https://www.fmscout.com/a-can-the-football-manager-ai-figure-out-your-tactic.html); [FM Scout Q&A](https://www.fmscout.com/q-21381-Tactic-longevity.html)
- GRF tactic properties per team (defence/offence depth, width, own-half factor, midfield focus, side focus) and dribble offensiveness / centre magnet, plus score/time-driven offensiveness. — [GRF teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp); [AIfunctions.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/AIsupport/AIfunctions.cpp)

### Inferences
- Map each style to GRF user tactic offsets: High Press = high `position_defense_depth_factor` offset plus larger hunt distance; Park the Bus = low defence depth, narrow defence width; Possession = higher midfield focus, lower dribble offensiveness; Wing Play = high offence width, low `dribble_centermagnet`; Counter/Kick and Rush = deep defence, high offence depth. Exact effect sizes need in-engine testing (offsets are bounded to +/-0.1 to 0.3).
- Like FC's opposition reacting to your reputation, raise opponents' defensiveness against a dominant user (big clubs sit deeper vs underdogs press) to keep a season varied without stat boosts.

### Gaps
- No source on how eFootball AI teams choose playstyles; no in-engine measurement of how far GRF tactic offsets visibly change play.
