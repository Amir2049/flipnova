# Player Locomotion Animation and Goalkeeper Behaviour in Football Games (for AFC: Godot 4.7, Android, GRF engine)

Scope note: tool budget was kept modest (about 15 calls). The strongest primary source here is the open GRF / GameplayFootball C++ source, which I cloned and read directly (sparse clone of github.com/google-research/football, `third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/...`). Commercial-game claims (EA, Konami) come mostly from marketing pages and press coverage, so they describe what the companies say, not verified internals. Line numbers refer to the master branch as cloned on 2026-11-24.

## Q1. How do top football games handle outfield locomotion (turning, accel/decel, foot planting, dribble touches, duels): motion matching vs blend trees vs procedural?

### Takeaway
AAA football moved from hand-authored blend trees and state machines to large mocap databases picked at runtime. EA's HyperMotion uses ML-Flow, trained on 11v11 Xsens match capture. Konami's eFootball (2021) uses motion matching. Both still rely on runtime "cheating" (root-motion and position correction) and blending. GRF/GameplayFootball, which AFC already runs on, is a hand-rolled precursor of motion matching. It filters and sorts about 300 mocap clips by incoming velocity tier, body direction, foot phase and desired movement, and it hides positional error with "smuggle" offsets. That is the main source of foot sliding. Inertialization-style blending plus foot IK is the standard fix.

### Cited Findings
**EA HyperMotion / ML-Flow (FIFA 22 onward)**
- ML-Flow was trained on motion capture of 22 players in Xsens suits playing full 11v11 matches. EA's figure is "8.7 million frames" of match capture, and some outlets say 7.8 million, so the sources conflict. The algorithm "studied how the players approach the ball, how many steps do they do to get to the ball, and then creates the animation in real time." EA cites it for aerial-ball takedowns and agility when trapping ground balls. It was limited to PS5, Xbox Series and Stadia. — [EA HyperMotion page](https://ea.com/games/fifa/fifa-22/hypermotion); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/ea-uses-machine-learning-for-animations-in-fifa-22); [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/news/hypermotion-technology-in-fifa-22-sounds-like-a-game-changer-but-what-is-it)
- HyperMotion contributed "over 4,000 new animations". FIFA 23's HyperMotion 2 kept the capture-plus-ML approach. — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/news/hypermotion-technology-in-fifa-22-sounds-like-a-game-changer-but-what-is-it); [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/hypermotion-2-in-fifa-23-how-machine-learning-drives-next-gen-animation-in-gaming)

**Konami eFootball**
- Producer Seitaro Kimura said realistic play needs both varied animations and high responsiveness, so Konami built "a completely new animation system", including "Motion Matching". It evaluates "the ball, movement speed, body direction, physical ability and more in real-time" and gives "more than four times as many animations as before". — [TechRadar interview](https://www.techradar.com/news/how-the-pro-evolution-of-konamis-efootball-came-to-be); [Konami press release via Mynewsdesk](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/uk/bastion-uk/pressreleases/new-efootball-gameplay-details-revealed-by-konami-at-gamescom-2021-3123618)
- Launch criticism: a shielding interaction where the attacker "bounce[d] off ... as if there's some invisible force field". Community complaints of stutter and sliding are anecdotal. — [Operation Sports](https://vip-develop.operationsports.com/efootball-gameplay-trailer-analysis-a-step-up-but-many-questions-remain); [Evoweb forum (anecdotal)](https://evoweb.uk/threads/why-pes-was-renamed-and-how-the-community-got-it-all-wrong.90583/)

**Motion matching, the technique**
- Simon Clavet (Ubisoft Montreal) presented "Motion Matching and The Road to Next-Gen Animation" at GDC 2016. For Honor was the first game to ship it. Kristjan Zadziuk (Ubisoft Toronto) also popularized the approach. One ResetEra post credits Zadziuk with the GDC 2016 talk, but the GDC Vault credits Clavet. — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022985/Motion-Matching-and-The-Road); [Ubisoft La Forge (DReCon page)](https://montreal.ubisoft.com/en/drecon-data-driven-responsive-control-of-physics-based-characters/); [ResetEra](https://www.resetera.com/threads/motion-matching-the-cutting-edge-technique-powering-the-last-of-us-2.49388/)
- Learned Motion Matching (Holden et al., 2020) compresses the database with neural networks to cut memory and search cost, which matters on mobile. When playback jumps frames, transitions use inertialization (Bollo, Gears of War 4), the industry-standard transition technique. — [arXiv 2602.21702 (Half Pound Filter, related work)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21702); [Learned Motion Matching summary](https://keneyr.github.io/Paper/Motion-Control/Paper-Learned-Motion-Matching/)
- Holden's "Dead Blending" is a cheaper alternative to inertialization. It notes that "foot-sliding and the general feeling of mushiness" come from bad cross-fades. — [theorangeduck.com Dead Blending](https://theorangeduck.com/page/dead-blending)
- Motion matching causes foot sliding when body motion does not match the character's root trajectory. — [arXiv 2510.22632](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22632v1)

**GRF / GameplayFootball: how it picks animations (read directly from source)**
- `HumanoidBase::SelectAnim` (humanoidbase.cpp ~L1112–1300) builds a `CrudeSelectionQuery`. It filters by function type (Movement, BallControl, Trap, ShortPass, Shot, Deflect, Sliding, Trip, Special...), by *strict* incoming velocity tier (idle/dribble/walk/sprint enum) and by *strict* incoming body direction, plus "incoming_special_state" (e.g., getting up from a fall) and "retain_state" (keeper holding the ball). It then stable-sorts the survivors in passes: desired movement direction and velocity (`_KeepBestDirectionAnims`, `GetMovementSimilarity` with a cornering bias), idle level, **foot similarity (preferring a clip whose current foot matches the outgoing foot of the previous clip)**, incoming body direction, incoming velocity, trip direction, and base-anim similarity. With no match, it falls back to an idle movement clip ("should not happen too often, only after weird bumps"). — [GRF source, humanoidbase.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/humanoid/humanoidbase.cpp)
- Clips are grouped into "quadrants" (velocity tier × angle). Requeuing (interrupting a running clip to change direction) is allowed only movement-to-movement and not into the same quadrant. That is GRF's version of a cancel window. — same file, ~L987–1060, L1234–1250
- Position error is hidden with "actionSmuggle", "movementSmuggle" and "rotationSmuggle" offsets added to the root each frame (humanoidbase.cpp L721–747). Ball touches are "cheated" toward the ball within a radius. The radius depends on touch frame (`touchFrameFactor = animTouchFrame/24`, favouring early-touch clips) and grows ×1.8 (+0.4 m) for keeper Deflect anims. Higher balls get more Z-cheat "else we would have to make 100000000 anims for high balls on different heights". — [GRF humanoid.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/humanoid/humanoid.cpp) (~L1930–2100)
- Dribble touch physics: the ideal contact point is 0.34 m in front of the foot plus a velocity-scaled term. Ball control velocity blends the command and current velocity 0.7/0.3. Touch difficulty scales with `(1 - ballcontrol*0.5) * random(0.5,1)`. Shots lose power when the clip's touch direction is more than 0.2π from the desired direction. — [GRF humanoid_utils.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/humanoid/humanoid_utils.cpp)

**Godot tooling**
- Godot 4.6 brought back IK as `IKModifier3D` subclasses of `SkeletonModifier3D`, including `TwoBoneIK3D` (deterministic two-bone solve with a pole target, the usual foot-planting tool) and `ChainIK3D`. 4.6 also added `BoneTwistDisperser3D` and `LimitAngularVelocityModifier3D`, for 19 new SkeletonModifiers from 4.4 to 4.6. — [Godot blog: IK returns in 4.6](https://godotengine.org/article/inverse-kinematics-returns-to-godot-4-6/); [TwoBoneIK3D docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_twoboneik3d.html)
- A forum user on 4.6.2 found foot IK worked once foot targets were updated *between* the AnimationMixer output and the IK modifier. The same user had trouble combining AnimationTree with SkeletonModifier3D. — [Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/help-setting-a-bone-pose/138445)

### Inferences
- AFC already has a primitive motion-matching selector: GRF's filter-and-sort over tagged clips. Re-architecting to full motion matching is probably not the best return for a solo developer. Foot sliding in AFC most likely comes from (a) smuggle offsets moving the root without moving the feet, (b) retargeting GRF clips to a MakeHuman body with different leg proportions, so stride length no longer matches root speed, and (c) cross-fading between clips with different foot phase.
- Cheapest high-impact fixes in Godot 4.7:
  1. Foot locking with `TwoBoneIK3D`. Detect plant frames (foot height and velocity below a threshold, or bake plant curves offline), pin the foot's world position while planted, and release with a short blend.
  2. Scale playback rate, or stride-warp, so animation root speed matches simulated speed instead of adding smuggle velocity.
  3. Replace linear cross-fades with inertialization or dead blending, which is easy to implement in a custom SkeletonModifier3D because Holden publishes simple code.
  4. Add procedural lean into turns (roll proportional to lateral acceleration) and a spine/head look-at toward the ball with `LookAtModifier3D`. Both are cheap and sell "naturalness".
- Keep GRF's discipline of matching on the foot phase (left/right outgoing foot). It is the single most important matching feature for stopping the "moonwalk" when changing clips.

### Gaps
- No primary EA technical detail was found on how ML-Flow runs at runtime (generative vs selection). EA material is marketing-level.
- No source found on how FC Mobile, eFootball mobile or Dream League Soccer simplify locomotion (clip counts, LOD animation, update-rate reduction). Searches were not spent here; treat as unknown.
- No verified per-game info on cancel windows or animation commitment (e.g., FC's "Agile Dribbling" or "Controlled Sprint"). Not researched within budget.

## Q2. Responsiveness vs realism, and mobile simplification

### Takeaway
Every source frames this as a trade-off. Konami says it needs both variety and responsiveness, and EA's ML-Flow aims for realistic step counts to the ball, which inherently adds commitment. GRF handles it with requeue rules (interrupt only movement-to-movement, after frame 12 recalculating spatial state) plus positional cheating. Mobile-specific evidence was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Kimura: realistic gameplay requires "a variety of animations and high responsiveness". Motion Matching was the answer. — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/how-the-pro-evolution-of-konamis-efootball-came-to-be)
- GRF recalculates the factual spatial state only if not requeuing or if `frameNum > 12` (120 ms at 10 ms per frame). Requeue is restricted to movement-to-movement with a different quadrant. — [GRF humanoidbase.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/humanoid/humanoidbase.cpp)
- EA FC 26 patches show live tuning of keeper responsiveness. The Holiday Update (v1.3.0) "considerably reduce[d] the movement speed and the amount of ground covered" by manually moved keepers and fixed keepers "react[ing] poorly to first time shots". — [Operation Sports](https://www.operationsports.com/how-does-ea-fc-26-feel-after-update-1-3-0); [Beebom](https://beebom.com/ea-fc-26-holiday-update-patch-notes-gk-movement-nerf-career-mode-improvements-and-more)

### Inferences
- On touch input, perceived responsiveness matters more than realism. Practical rule: allow direction-change requeues within about 100–150 ms for dribble/walk tiers, keep longer commitment for sprint and turn clips, and fake the rest with rotation smuggle and procedural lean. GRF's 120 ms threshold is a reasonable starting point.

### Gaps
- No source on DLS, FC Mobile or eFootball-mobile animation pipelines. Recommend a targeted search or teardown if this matters.

## Q3. Goalkeeper: positioning, reactions, dives, saves, fairness

### Takeaway
The accepted geometric model puts the keeper on the bisector of the shooter–post angle and slightly off the line. Coming off the line narrows the angle but risks being chipped or losing the sense of the line. GRF implements exactly this, plus a rush-out rule. Its catch-vs-parry decision is a simple product of ball-speed difficulty and reaction difficulty. Real dive timing (about 0.5 s to decide, about 0.6 s to reach a side, about 1 s for a top corner) gives a physical basis for save-probability tuning.

### Cited Findings
**Real-world positioning and timing**
- The optimal position lies on the bisector of the angle from the shooting position to the two posts, so the keeper is equidistant from both sides. Leaving the line gives a larger angle to parry the ball out, but too far erodes awareness of the goal line. — [Barańska & Eckes (ejournals.eu)](https://ejournals.eu/pliki_artykulu_czasopisma/pelny_tekst/ac1024df-c6ab-44fb-af2b-61f58325dbb5/pobierz)
- When a keeper stands slightly off-centre, penalty takers send about 60% of shots to the larger side, even when they do not consciously notice. — [Noel et al. 2015](https://www.dshs-koeln.de/fileadmin/redaktion/Institute/Kognitions-_und_Sportspielforschung/Publikationen/Paper/Noel_2015_Implicit-Goalkeeper-Influences-on-Goal-Side-Selection-in-Representative-Penalty-Kicking-Tasks.pdf)
- At the World Cup 2022, keepers stood 3.6 m higher up the pitch than in 2018 when their team was in possession in the final third. — [FIFA Training Centre](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/fwc2022/technical-and-tactical-analysis/goalkeeper-height-and-connection-with-the-defensive-line.php)
- Informal StatsBomb freeze-frame analysis suggests keepers should tilt slightly toward the near post. — [znstrider blog](https://znstrider.github.io/2018-11-08-Analysing-the-Importance-of-Shot-Angles)
- Dive timing: about 0.5 s after the kick to choose a side, about 0.6 s to reach a side, about 1 s to stretch to a top corner. This comes from a news report on van der Kamp's model, not the paper itself. Lab studies fire balls that reach the goal in about 1.2 s to measure reaction time plus movement time. — [TheJournal.ie](https://www.thejournal.ie/article.php?id=3973669); [Frontiers 2020 (Ibrahim et al.)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2020.00013/pdf)
- A wider ready stance (about 75% of leg length) gives the fastest dive movement time and a more direct centre-of-mass path. — [Faber & van Dieën 2019 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7739716)

**GRF goalie implementation (read directly from `goalie_default.cpp` and the controllers)**
- Lateral: posts at y = ±3.7 m. The keeper's target is on the bisector of the ball-to-posts angle, clamped to the goal width (L82). — [goalie_default.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/strategies/offtheball/goalie_default.cpp)
- Depth: `awayFromGoalOffset_m = 0.7` minimum along the bisector. `awayFromGoalBias = 0.3 * NormalizedClamp(teamPossession, 1.0, 1.5)`, so the bias is 0 when the opponent has the ball and 0.3 when his own team does. Final depth is `clamp((ballToGoalDist - 0.5) * bias, 0.7, pitchHalfW)` (L85–87, L166). So while defending, the keeper sits about 0.7 m off the line, and he steps up to 30% of the ball distance when his team attacks.
- Rush-out: the opponent's position is predicted 0.32 s ahead. If the closest teammate is more than 1 m farther from the "shoot threshold" point (20 m, or 24 m for a secondary opponent) than the attacker, `bias = 1.0`, meaning the keeper runs all the way to the ball along the bisector. A dangerous secondary attacker reduces this (L109–163). A manual "keeper rush" button also forces `bias = 1`.
- Ball bound for goal: a 2D line from the ball now to the ball at +800 ms is intersected with the goal line, and it counts as on target if |y| ≤ 3.7 × panic, where `panic = 1.02 + (1 - (defPos*0.6 + vision*0.4)) * 0.5`. Low-rated keepers therefore react to more off-target shots. The 3D height test is commented out. The check needs the keeper within 32 m of the ball (L170–230 region).
- When the ball is bound for goal, the keeper moves to the closest point on the ball's path (offset 0.4 m toward the ball). If the ball will be above 2.5 m on arrival, he retreats to the goal line instead.
- The "dive" is not scripted. The AI issues `e_FunctionType_Deflect` commands, and the Humanoid selector picks a Deflect clip whose touch point can be cheated to the ball (radius ×1.8, +0.4 m, Z ×0.8). Clips are sorted by `CompareCatchOrDeflect`. The keeper's mental image for deflections is "near-instant; let's just call it premonition ;)" (elizacontroller.cpp L354). Everyone else uses `reaction_ms = 80 - reaction_stat*40` (icontroller.cpp L32). — [elizacontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp); [icontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/icontroller.cpp)
- Catch vs parry: catching is possible only if the clip has an `outgoing_retain_state`. `veloDifficulty = NormalizedClamp(|ballVel - keeperVel|, 0, 40 m/s)`. `reactionDifficulty = pow(lastShooterTouchBias(1200 - reaction*400 ms), 0.6)`. The keeper catches if `(1-velo)*(1-reaction) ≥ 0.3`, otherwise the ball is deflected (humanoid.cpp L563–579). The model is deterministic: there is no random save roll. Saves depend on whether a clip can reach the ball.

**Commercial games and fairness**
- EA FC 26 patches repeatedly tuned keepers "for more authentic play based on feedback", nerfed manual keeper movement speed and range, and fixed poor reactions to first-time shots and keepers interacting wrongly with the net. — [Operation Sports](https://www.operationsports.com/how-does-ea-fc-26-feel-after-update-1-3-0); [Beebom](https://beebom.com/ea-fc-26-holiday-update-patch-notes-gk-movement-nerf-career-mode-improvements-and-more); [Dot Esports v1.2.0](https://dotesports.com/?p=1571370)

### Inferences (applied to AFC's test: 21 dives on 29 shots, 15 saves, keeper 1.6–2 m off the line when beaten)
- 52% saves (15/29) is high but not absurd. Real-world save percentage on shots on target is typically around 70%, but that figure is from general knowledge and was not sourced here. AFC should measure saves per shot on target, broken down by xG band, rather than raw saves per shot.
- The 1.6–2 m depth when beaten is consistent with GRF's logic. The minimum is 0.7 m along the bisector. A from-angle bisector point also moves out, and the rush rule (`bias=1`) or a possession value between 1.0 and 1.5 pushes the keeper further. Being beaten while 1.6–2 m out and mid-transition is typical of chips and near-post shots. Options: cap depth by shot distance (e.g., ≤ 1.0–1.5 m when the shooter is inside 18 m and not 1v1), make the keeper set (stop and widen stance) before the shot when the shooter's touch indicates a shot, and slow the backwards move near the line (GRF already uses walk speed within 1 m).
- Diving on 21 of 29 shots (72%) suggests the "bound for goal" panic factor plus the 2D (height-less) test triggers on off-target shots. Re-enable a height and post check with a margin of about 0.3 m. Low-rated keepers can keep the wider panic margin to stay "human".
- Fairness model to adopt: deterministic reachability (can a dive clip's hand reach the ball's intercept point within reaction time + clip time?) plus a skill-scaled catch/parry quality roll. Use real timing constraints for reaction delay (about 150–250 ms for AI keepers, not GRF's near-zero "premonition") and reach time (about 0.6 s side, about 1 s top corner). This yields natural "unsavable" top-corner shots and avoids superhuman keepers. Expose the constants per difficulty level.
- Make parries direct the ball wide or over (re-entrant angle) rather than back into the centre. The geometric paper notes that coming off the line increases the parry-redirect angle.
- Manual keeper control on mobile: the FC 26 nerf suggests manual keeper movement must be speed- and range-limited, or users "cheese" by pre-moving.

### Gaps
- Crosses and claiming, plus distribution (throws, kicks, quick restarts): not researched within budget. GRF's `onlyDeflectAnimsThatPickupBall` flag (when possession > 1.3 and the ball is not bound for goal) handles loose-ball pickup, but cross-claiming logic was not examined.
- No peer-reviewed save-probability model (e.g., post-shot xG / PSxG formulas) was retrieved. Recommend a follow-up search on StatsBomb PSxG or "goals saved above expected" models.
- No source on how FC Mobile or eFootball mobile tune keepers.
- GRF clip count ("~300") and the exact list of keeper dive clips were not verified from data files (animation data lives outside the C++ tree).
