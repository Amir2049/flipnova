# Mobile Football Look: Rendering, Presentation and Performance Tiers (for a solo Godot 4.7 Android developer)

Research date: 2026-11-24. Searches were limited (about 15 tool calls). Several primary pages (EA help centre, EA forums blog repost, Unreal Engine spotlight, UEFA lighting guide body text) returned 403/503 or image-only content, so some points rely on search snippets of forum posts. Each item says how reliable it is.

## 1. Graphics settings and tiers in mobile football games, and what low tiers reduce

### Takeaway
FC Mobile and eFootball both expose separate quality and frame-rate settings, and high frame rates are gated per device by a whitelist rather than by spec alone. eFootball's lowest stadium tier removes spectators and commentary, and FC Mobile exposes per-effect toggles (DOF, Bloom, MSAA) plus a separate resolution setting. No official tier tables were found for any of the four games.

### Cited Findings
- **FC Mobile menu items (player reports):** Graphics Quality (presets up to at least "Ultra"), a separate Resolution setting (one value is "High"), toggles for DOF, Bloom and MSAA, and a Frame Rate menu that on some devices shows only "Standard"/"Enable" instead of 60/90/120 — [EA Forums, POCO F6 thread](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-mobile-technical-issues-en/poco-f6-snapdragon-8s-gen-3---no-6090120-fps-options/13640012); [EA Forums, 60/90/120 FPS unavailable thread](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-mobile-technical-issues-en/fc-mobile-6090120-fps-or-graphics-settings-unavailable-on-specific-devices/13277114/replies/13640012)
- **FC Mobile 60/90/120 FPS is device-gated.** Players with strong chipsets (Snapdragon 8s Gen 3, 12 GB RAM) report the high-FPS options missing. A POCO X7 Pro shows 120 FPS but locked even on Ultra. A Redmi Note 14 4G (Helio G99, 6 GB) owner asked to be added to a 60 FPS whitelist — [EA Forums POCO F6](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-mobile-technical-issues-en/poco-f6-snapdragon-8s-gen-3---no-6090120-fps-options/13640012); [EA Forums POCO X7 Pro](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-mobile-it/unlocking-very-high-frame-rate-option-for-the-poco-x7-pro/13609628); [EA Forums Redmi Note 14](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-mobile-technical-issues-en/request-to-enable-60-fps-support-on-xiaomi-redmi-note-14-4g/13804707)
- **FC Mobile quality presets are also capped per device.** One user had Ultra and everything maxed on a Snapdragon 778G (A52s) but was restricted on a newer phone. Another noted a Snapdragon 685 phone was offered better presets than theirs — [EA Forums "why is graphic options restricted"](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-mobile-bug-reports-en/why-is-graphic-options-restricted/13222392); [EA Forums Exynos vs Snapdragon](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-mobile-bug-reports-en/graphics-settings---exynos-vs-snapdragon/8027511)
- EA has an official "EA SPORTS FC Mobile system requirements" help article, but it could not be fetched (HTTP 503) — [EA Help (JA)](https://help.ea.com/ja/articles/ea-sports-fc/fc-mobile/system-requirements/)
- **eFootball mobile has three settings:** Player Graphics, Stadium Graphics and Frame Rate (30/60). The old "Low" Player Graphics preset has been removed. The lowest Stadium Graphics preset, "Lowest (Prioritize Playability)", **removes spectators and disables commentary**. 60 FPS is a target, not a guarantee, and 30 FPS is steadier on weak phones — [Appuals, eFootball 2026 mobile settings](https://appuals.com/best-efootball-2026-mobile-settings/)
- eFootball guide advice is 60 FPS with "Standard" Player and Stadium graphics, and "High" only on high-end devices — [GamingonPhone guide](https://gamingonphone.com/guides/efootball-guide-to-the-best-gameplay-and-game-plan-settings-for-a-smooth-experience/)
- **Engines:** eFootball (mobile included) runs on Unreal Engine (Konami spotlight for PES 2020 Mobile). FC Mobile is reported to run on EA's Impact engine, not Unity — [Unreal Engine spotlight (title/snippet only, page 403)](https://www.unrealengine.com/spotlights/unreal-engine-spotlight-konami-s-efootball-pes-2020-mobile); [Wikipedia: EA Sports FC Mobile](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Mobile_(2016)); [TechRadar on eFootball's move to Unreal](https://www.techradar.com/news/how-the-pro-evolution-of-konamis-efootball-came-to-be)
- **FC 26 console/PC (accessibility):** FC 26 added separate toggles for **player shadows** and **stadium shadows**, because pitch shadows made players and the ball hard to see in some lighting — [EA Pitch Notes FC 26 Gameplay Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/pitch-notes-fc26-gameplay-deep-dive) (content seen through a search snippet of the forum repost; direct fetch 403)

### Inferences
- The shared low-tier pattern is to cut the crowd first (eFootball removes it entirely), then post effects (DOF, bloom, MSAA), then resolution scale, and to keep the gameplay camera and pitch intact. Device whitelisting is how big studios keep 60 FPS from being unstable, which a solo developer cannot maintain. A simpler equivalent is an automatic benchmark in the first match that drops a tier if frame time exceeds the budget, plus a manual override.
- Suggested AFC tiers in Godot 4.7 (Mobile renderer). **Low:** 30 FPS, 3D render scale about 0.7, no MSAA (or FXAA), no glow, crowd as a single static textured shell, stadium shadow baked into the pitch texture or a decal, blob shadows under players. **Medium:** 60 FPS, scale about 0.85, MSAA 2x, animated card crowd (MultiMesh) on the lower tiers only, one directional shadow cascade limited to the pitch. **High:** 60 (or 90/120 where `DisplayServer.screen_get_refresh_rate()` allows), scale 1.0, MSAA 4x, light glow, full card crowd and real-time player shadows.
- Making stadium shadows optional (as FC 26 does) is both an accessibility and a performance knob.

### Gaps
- No official Dream League Soccer 2026 or Football League 2026 settings documentation was found. The App Store listing for DLS 2026 says nothing about graphics options ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1462911602)), and the Sportsdunia "DLS 2026 best settings" page returned only navigation text.
- No official FC Mobile device list or per-preset breakdown of what each preset changes (crowd count, shadow resolution, player LOD) was found. The EA help article was unreachable.
- No First Touch Games (DLS) technical talk or Unity case study was found.

## 2. Presentation elements that make EA FC broadcasts look "real"

### Takeaway
Real TV football is shot from one main camera on the halfway line, high in the main stand, looking down at roughly 12–16° to the pitch centre. Games copy that framing ("Tele Broadcast"), and it is the single biggest realism cue. EA's own FC 26/27 material says little about broadcast presentation beyond stadiums, lighting/shadows and crowd animation.

### Cited Findings
- **Main camera geometry (broadcast standard):** the main camera platform is ideally 30–40 m back from the near sideline. It sits at a height that gives a 20–30° angle down to the near sideline and **12–16° down to the pitch centre**, for example 16–22 m above the pitch at 40 m back. It must clear standing fans, and coverage suffers beyond 60 m distance or at excessive height — [Free TV Australia OP-32 TV coverage plan (soccer)](https://www.freetv.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/OP-32-Outside-broadcast-requirements-for-rugby-league-rugby-union-and-soccer-Issue-4-January-2015..pdf)
- Offside cameras sit at the same height and angle as the main cameras, and high-behind-goal cameras are placed so the penalty mark is visible above the crossbar — [Free TV OP-32](https://www.freetv.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/OP-32-Outside-broadcast-requirements-for-rugby-league-rugby-union-and-soccer-Issue-4-January-2015..pdf); [UEFA Europa League regs E.2.7](https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Europa-League-2023/24/E.2.7-High-behind-goal-cameras-Online)
- The main camera platform sits on the halfway line in the main stand, placed so the sun does not shine into the cameras. In practice that means the camera usually looks toward the shaded side (it faces away from the setting sun) — [FIFA Stadium Guidelines 6.2](https://publications.fifa.com/de/football-stadiums-guidelines/technical-guideline/main-user-groups/media-and-broadcast/); [UEFA WCL regs E.2.1 Main cameras](https://documents.uefa.com/r/Regulations-of-the-UEFA-Women-s-Champions-League-2025/26/E.2.1-Main-cameras-Online)
- Ground-level camera positions include cameras behind the byline (3–5 m back) and dead-ball-corner tripods 5 m behind the line. These are the angles replays cut to — [Free TV OP-32](https://www.freetv.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/OP-32-Outside-broadcast-requirements-for-rugby-league-rugby-union-and-soccer-Issue-4-January-2015..pdf)
- **FC 26 presentation claims (secondary blog, low reliability):** refined lighting, upgraded stadium lighting and shadows, upgraded crowd animations, "more responsive camera angles", smoother transitions, and a style meant to feel like a televised broadcast. Also more detailed sideline managers — [1v1Me blog](https://www.1v1me.com/blog/fc-26-visual-upgrades-seasonal-presentation)
- Player complaints about FC 26 Tele Broadcast and Co-op cameras say upper-deck roof and lighting elements block the top of the screen, and that this has been an issue "since FIFA 15". The camera framing that shows roof structure in frame is a known trade-off — [EA Forums bug report](https://forums.ea.com/idea/fc-26-bug-reports-en/broadcast-camera-blocking-pitch/13076324/comments/13078718)
- FC 26 stadium list: new stadiums such as Allianz Arena are "rebuilt from scratch". EA invests in stadium fidelity as a headline feature — [EA: New FC 26 stadiums](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/fc-26-all-new-stadiums-list)
- FC 27 (released September 2026): a review praises "packed stadiums" and an atmosphere that captures "the spectacle of a real match". No official broadcast or presentation deep dive was found — [Maxim FC 27 review](https://www.maxim.com/sports/ea-sports-fc-27-review-the-game-formerly-known-as-fifa-feels-beautiful-again/); [EA FC 27 features](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-27/features)
- Unity's Top Eleven case study (Nordeus, 3D live matches on mobile/WebGL) mentions using Cinemachine to iterate on match cameras. The tooling idea is to make the broadcast camera data-driven and easy to tune — [Unity case study: Top Eleven](https://activation.unity3d.com/cn/case-study/top-eleven)

### Inferences
- **Camera recipe for AFC (derived from OP-32):** put the camera on the halfway line, about 35–40 m behind the near touchline and about 18–20 m high. That gives about 12–15° pitch to the centre circle. Use a long lens: in Godot, a vertical FOV around 20–30° (zoomed), not 60–70°. Pan to follow the ball while keeping the halfway-line position, and change only zoom (FOV) and yaw, as a real fixed-tripod camera does. The flat, compressed telephoto look is a major "TV" cue that low-cost games often miss by using a wide FOV and a camera close behind the play.
- Pitch-side elements that cost little but read as "real": an LED advertising board ring (one emissive mesh strip with a scrolling texture), dugouts and technical areas, photographers behind the goal lines (a few low-poly or card figures), stewards facing the crowd, the fourth official's board, and a running track or ad-board gap. All of these can be MultiMesh billboards or static meshes with a shared atlas.
- In broadcast framing the near touchline, ad boards and first crowd rows are always in the bottom or top of frame, so they deserve the most detail. The far stand is mostly a texture.

### Gaps
- No primary EA source (Pitch Notes or a developer talk) describing FC 26/27 broadcast cameras, replays, intros or the HUD was found. I found no Digital Foundry analysis of FC 26/27 presentation.
- The UEFA Stadium Lighting Guide 2023 TV camera plan is an image and could not be read. Its sections on player shadows and uniformity exist but were not extracted ([UEFA guide TV camera plan](https://documents.uefa.com/r/UEFA-Stadium-Lighting-Guide-2023-2023/TV-camera-plan-Online)).
- No source was found on scoreboard/HUD minimalism, replay structure or commentary systems in FC 26/27.

## 3. Developer techniques: mobile crowd rendering, grass, engines

### Takeaway
The standard mobile crowd technique is instanced low-poly or 2D card (impostor) spectators with animation baked into textures, using compressed atlases. Close rows may use meshes and distant ones impostors. Real pitch stripes come from light reflecting off grass blades bent in alternating mowing directions, not from paint or cut height, so a shader brightness and normal band is the physically correct imitation.

### Cited Findings
- A stadium-crowd chapter in a GPU rendering book (CRC/Taylor & Francis) covers instancing, deferred rendering and impostors for crowds, and stresses accurate seat and character placement — [Taylor & Francis chapter](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/9780203705179/chapters/10.1201/b22483-9)
- A hybrid LOD approach (Dobbyn et al.) uses polygon meshes for nearby characters and pre-generated impostors for distant ones — [Springer paper](https://rd.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-540-89222-9_24.pdf)
- Space Ape (mobile, Unity) stayed with **2D crowds** because thousands of skinned meshes were too expensive on OpenGL ES 2.0-class devices — [Space Ape Tech: Crowd rendering on mobile with Unity](https://medium.com/spaceapetech/crowd-rendering-on-mobile-with-unity-b14745126a2a) (article body 403; summary from search snippet)
- Unity forum advice for stadium fans on mobile: low-poly characters plus GPU instancing with **animation baked into a texture** — [Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/what-is-the-best-way-to-make-fans-cheering-in-a-stadium-for-a-mobile-game/883782)
- A gamedev.net thread about a roughly 10,000-seat stadium recommends the most compressed texture formats on mobile to save memory — [gamedev.net thread](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/703015-efficient-crowd-audience-rendering/)
- Geometry instancing merges many draws of the same mesh into one draw call, which cuts CPU overhead on Android — [Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2015/05/game-performance-geometry-instancing.html?hl=en)
- **Pitch stripes:** stripes appear because light reflects differently off blades bent toward or away from the viewer. They are not painted and not created by different cut heights. Rollers bend blades further to sharpen the effect — [Asian Turfgrass: stripes after mowing](https://www.asianturfgrass.com/post/stripes-after-mowing-not-just-grass/); [Sod Solutions](https://sodsolutions.com/sod-university/how-to-stripe-your-lawn-like-a-sports-field/); [Swardman guide](https://www.swardman.com/en/tutorials/football-pitch-lawn-stripes)

### Inferences
- **Crowd for Godot:** one `MultiMeshInstance3D` per stand section with quads (cards) facing the camera axis, or fixed-facing since the broadcast camera barely moves. Use a texture atlas of 8–16 fan poses with 2–4 frame bounce animation driven in the vertex shader (`INSTANCE_CUSTOM` for random phase, shirt-colour tint and pose index). Use `alpha_scissor`, not alpha blend, for mobile overdraw. Team-colour tinting per instance gives home/away fan sections. Low tier replaces cards with a painted stand texture, which mirrors eFootball's "remove spectators" tier.
- **Grass for Godot:** a single pitch plane with an albedo detail texture plus a shader stripe term. Use `floor(world_x / stripe_width)` parity, with stripe width about 5–6 m (a common pattern; not sourced). Light and dark bands change with view direction relative to the mowing direction, for example `mix(dark, light, parity)` modulated by `dot(view_dir_xz, mow_dir)`, so stripes shift as the camera pans, as on TV. Add low-frequency noise for wear and patchiness, and slightly darker, more worn goalmouths and centre circle.
- Because the broadcast camera is far and at a low angle, geometric grass blades are wasted on mobile. Albedo, stripe and noise variation plus slight anisotropic specular (or roughness variation) per band is enough.

### Gaps
- No talk or article from EA (FC Mobile) or Konami (eFootball mobile) on their actual crowd or grass implementation was found. The Unreal spotlight page was unreachable (403).
- No First Touch Games or Football League (MOBILE SOCCER) technical material was found.

## 4. TV football colour grading conventions games emulate

### Takeaway
Broadcast lighting standards fix floodlight colour temperature near 5600 K (4000–6500 K allowed) with high colour rendering and even illumination. Broadcasters then apply a "traditional sports look" of extra saturation and tone/contrast on top of a neutral camera image. Strong sun/shade contrast across the pitch is a recognised defining feature of daytime football pictures. No source gives numerical grading targets for grass hue or saturation.

### Cited Findings
- **Floodlight spec for TV:** about 1400 lux (average) toward the main cameras for international-standard venues and 1000 lux for professional standard. Colour temperature is 4000–6500 K, within 500 K per venue, **preferred 5600 K**. Colour rendering index Ra ≥ 90 (minimum 85) — [Free TV OP-32 §9](https://www.freetv.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/OP-32-Outside-broadcast-requirements-for-rugby-league-rugby-union-and-soccer-Issue-4-January-2015..pdf)
- **BBC Euro 2020 UHD/HDR:** HLG lets the creative team keep a natural image or add "saturation and tone controls to achieve other artistic intents, such as the traditional sports look". Kits are noted as "very saturated" — [SVG Europe: BBC Euro 2020 UHD HDR](https://www.svgeurope.org/blog/headlines/how-the-bbc-designed-uhd-hdr-for-euro-2020-islands-in-the-sun/)
- Graphics (score bug, flags) must not change brightness or colour when the action moves between sun and shade, so HUD is kept independent of scene exposure — [SVG Europe: BBC Euro 2020](https://www.svgeurope.org/blog/headlines/how-the-bbc-designed-uhd-hdr-for-euro-2020-islands-in-the-sun/)
- Matching colour between cameras is critical in live broadcast, so the whole match has one consistent look — [SVG Europe (Red Digital Cinema)](https://www.svgeurope.org/blog/headlines/uhd-is-dead-long-live-uhd-the-future-of-live-sports-broadcast-relies-on-uhd-adoption-says-red/)
- A typical football picture has stark contrast between the brightly lit pitch and shadowed stands or sidelines. HDR is sold on handling that contrast and on letting kits pop against the grass — [TV Answer Man: football in HDR](https://tvanswerman.com/2023/09/13/football-in-hdr-6-ways-it-makes-the-picture-so-much-better/)
- Grass green benefits from wide colour gamut. Real grass contains colour slightly outside Rec.709, so SDR pictures compress it — [Red Bee Media](https://www.redbeemedia.com/?p=8777); [SVG Europe FutureSPORT 2018](https://www.svgeurope.org/blog/headlines/futuresport-2018-new-directions-for-high-dynamic-range-in-live-sports-production/)
- Daylight shadows on the pitch: FC 26 added stadium-shadow toggles because players complained that pitch shadows hurt visibility. This confirms the game renders hard roof shadows on the pitch by default — [EA FC 26 Gameplay Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/pitch-notes-fc26-gameplay-deep-dive)

### Inferences (applied to AFC's measured numbers)
- AFC's grass (mean hue ~101°, saturation ~0.54, luminance SD ~17) is bluer, less saturated and flatter than FC 27 frames (hue 65–90°, saturation 0.66–0.73, luminance SD 20–25). This matches the "traditional sports look": warm yellow-green, saturated, with contrast. A practical sequence in Godot:
  1. Shift grass albedo toward yellow-green (target hue about 75–85°), for example sRGB around (0.30, 0.50, 0.15) as a starting point to tune.
  2. Use a warm-neutral sun of about 5600 K equivalent: slightly warm white `Color(1.0, 0.96, 0.9)`. Godot 4 also has a light temperature property when physical light units are enabled.
  3. In `WorldEnvironment`, use `tonemap_mode = Filmic` or AgX, and adjustments with saturation about 1.1–1.2 and contrast about 1.05–1.1, or a small 3D colour-correction LUT (`adjustment_color_correction`) authored by grading a FC reference frame. All of these are cheap on the Mobile renderer.
  4. Raise luminance variance with stripes, wear noise and, most of all, a **stadium roof shadow across part of the pitch**. A sunlit/shaded split alone moves luminance SD substantially, and it is the most recognisable daytime TV cue.
- **Cheap roof shadow:** do not use real-time shadow maps for the stadium. Bake a projected shadow mask (a texture in pitch UV space, or a `Decal` node) whose edge position follows sun direction per stadium/time preset. Multiply grass albedo or lighting by about 0.45–0.6 in shadow with a slightly cooler, bluer tint. This costs almost nothing and gives FC's look on low tiers. Keep real-time shadows only for players (or blob or projected-quad shadows on Low).
- Keep the HUD (score bug, timer) small, top-left, flat colours, unaffected by tonemapping (CanvasLayer), consistent with broadcast practice.

- No published broadcaster grading spec (target grass hue, saturation or contrast values) was found. The numeric targets above come from the user's own FC 27 frame measurements plus my inference.
- No source quantified how much EA FC applies grading or LUTs versus physically based lighting.
